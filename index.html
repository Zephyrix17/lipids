Lipids

Lipids are the fats, oils and waxes in your food and your body.

What are lipids?

Lipids are fats, oils and waxes. They don’t mix with water.

Think of oil in salad dressing. It floats on top and won’t blend with the water.

Scientists call this hydrophobic (it doesn’t mix with water).

You already know some lipids. Butter and cooking oil are lipids. So is the fat in your body.

The four types of lipids

Lipids come in four main types. Tap a tab to meet each one.

Triglycerides: the fat we eat and store

Triglycerides are the fat in your food. Your body also stores them as body fat for later energy.

  • Three fat chains hang off one small backbone.
  • Butter and cooking oil are mostly triglycerides.
  • Your body burns them when it needs energy.

Phospholipids: the “skin” around every cell

Every cell in your body is wrapped in a thin skin. That skin is made of phospholipids.

  • Each one has a head that likes water.
  • Each one also has two tails that avoid water.
  • They line up in a double layer, with the tails tucked inside.

Steroids: cholesterol and some hormones

Steroids look different from other lipids. They are built from four joined rings, not long chains.

  • Cholesterol is one well-known kind of steroid.
  • Your body uses it to make some hormones (chemical messengers).
  • It also helps keep the skin around cells flexible.

Waxes: waterproof coatings

Waxes make a waterproof coating, like the shiny layer on a leaf. Your ears make a wax too, called earwax.

  • Water beads up and rolls off a waxy surface.
  • Bees build honeycomb from beeswax.
  • Earwax is a waxy mix that helps protect your ears.

What lipids do for you

Store energy

Fat is your body’s spare fuel tank. Gram for gram, it holds more than twice the energy of carbs or protein.

Protect organs

Body fat cushions your organs, like bubble wrap around a gift.

Keep you warm

A layer of fat under your skin holds in body heat, like a cozy blanket.

Build cells

Lipids form the skin around every cell. It keeps each cell’s insides in place.

Help you use vitamins A, D, E and K

These vitamins dissolve in fat, not water. The fat you eat helps your body take them in.

Saturated vs. unsaturated fat

Fats are made of long chains. The shape of the chain changes how the fat behaves.

Quick recap

  • Lipids are fats, oils and waxes. They don’t mix with water.
  • There are four types: triglycerides, phospholipids, steroids and waxes.
  • Lipids store energy, cushion organs, keep you warm and build cells.
  • Straight chains stack and make solid fats. Bent chains make liquid oils.
